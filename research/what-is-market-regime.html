MARKET FRAMEWORK

What Is a Market Regime?

Markets move through different environments. Understanding these changes helps investors evaluate risk and allocation decisions.

Markets Do Not Behave the Same Way Forever

Many investors describe markets using simple categories: bull market, bear market, risk-on, risk-off. However, market behavior is influenced by multiple forces that change over time.

Economic growth, inflation, liquidity conditions, central bank policy and market risk interact to create different investment environments. These environments are often described as market regimes.

What Is a Market Regime?

A market regime is a period where a specific combination of economic and financial conditions influences how different assets perform.

Key Regime Drivers

  • Growth conditions
  • Inflation trends
  • Liquidity environment
  • Policy conditions
  • Market risk

Why Market Regimes Matter for Investors

Different environments can create different opportunities and risks. For example:

Growth Expansion

Improving economic activity may support risk assets.

Reflation

Growth recovery combined with changing inflation conditions can alter asset leadership.

Risk Contraction

Higher uncertainty and deteriorating conditions may require more defensive positioning.

Sovereign Stress

Extreme conditions may require increased focus on capital preservation.

From Market Regime to Portfolio Decisions

Understanding the current regime is only the first step. Investors also need to evaluate:

  • How much risk should the portfolio take?
  • Should exposure increase or decrease?
  • Which asset categories are supported by current conditions?
